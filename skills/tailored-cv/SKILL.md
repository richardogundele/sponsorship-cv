# Tailored CV

Turn the user's existing CV into a CV written for one specific job. Every line must be true and must come from what the user has told you.

## What you need

1. **The user's current CV** (pasted text or an uploaded file), or enough detail about their work history to build one.
2. **The job description** for the role they are applying to.

If either is missing, ask for it before writing. Ask only for information you actually need.

## Workflow

1. **Read the job description.** List the 5 to 8 things the employer most needs: core responsibilities, must-have skills, tools, qualifications and the problems the role exists to solve. Note the exact words the advert uses.
2. **Map the evidence.** For each requirement, find where the user's CV already proves it. Mark each one as **strong**, **weak** (present but vague) or **missing**.
3. **Ask about gaps before writing.** For weak or missing items, ask short, specific questions ("Did you lead that migration, or support it? Roughly how many users did it affect?"). Do not fill gaps with guesses.
4. **Write the CV** using the structure below. Rewrite only from facts the user has given. Where a number or detail would help but you don't have it, leave a clearly marked placeholder such as `[number of customers]` rather than inventing one.
5. **Finish with a check list** (see Output).

## CV structure (UK conventions)

- **Name and contact line:** name, town or city, phone, email, LinkedIn. No photo, date of birth, age, marital status or nationality.
- **Profile:** 3 to 4 lines written for this role, naming the job title and the 2 or 3 strengths the advert cares about most.
- **Key skills:** 6 to 10 skills, using the advert's own wording where it truthfully describes the user.
- **Experience:** most recent first. Job title, employer, location, dates. 3 to 6 bullet points per recent role, each starting with a strong verb and showing action plus result ("Reduced ticket backlog by 40% by introducing..."). Older roles can be shortened.
- **Education and certifications:** most recent first.
- **Optional:** projects, volunteering or publications, only if relevant to this role.

Keep it to **two pages** for most candidates (one page for early-career). Use UK spelling and plain formatting that applicant tracking systems can read: no tables, columns, text boxes, icons or graphics.

## Right to work and sponsorship

Only mention immigration status if the user asks you to. If they do, write one short, factual line using exactly what they tell you (for example "Graduate visa valid until June 2027; will require Skilled Worker sponsorship"). Never suggest hiding, blurring or misstating someone's immigration status.

## Rules

- **Never invent** employers, job titles, dates, qualifications, achievements, numbers or skills.
- Match the advert's language only where it is true for the user.
- Prefer specific evidence and results over generic claims like "hard-working team player".
- Do not promise interviews, passing an applicant tracking system, sponsorship or a visa outcome.

## Output

1. The finished CV in clean plain text, ready to paste into a document.
2. **Requirement match:** a short list showing which advert requirements the CV now covers, and any that are still weak.
3. **Check before you send:** placeholders to fill, facts to double-check and any questions you still have.
